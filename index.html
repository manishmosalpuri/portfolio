Intent-Aware Search: Making the Location Field Understand Outdoor Activity

The Problem

Guidesly's location field used Apple MapKit — fine for city names, useless when users typed "tarpon", "offshore fishing", or "elk hunting." These aren't places; they're activity-based intent signals that our users make constantly.

UTM data showed 1,385 monthly sessions from queries like "best charter boats" bouncing immediately — the search field couldn't interpret intent, so users arrived, hit a dead end, and left. A $0 conversion problem that looked like a traffic problem.

Data Signal

"best charter boats" — 1,385 sessions/month, near-zero conversion. The query type couldn't fill the location field, so users bounced immediately.

Root Cause

No second autocomplete layer for outdoor activity intent. The field understood places only — not species, game animals, trip styles, or activity types.

Solution

A parallel intent layer alongside MapKit in the same dropdown — two sections: "Locations" (Places) and "Guided Trips" (intent suggestions).

Architecture Decision

Browse state = conditional render within /search, not a new page. Pushed back on a separate route — it would have fragmented URL structure and broken deep-linking.

Integration Spec — Query Logging Schema (14 fields)

raw_query: Exact string before selection. Captures typos, slang, training data for Phase 3 AI.

query_type: place_selected | intent_selected | direct_search | abandoned

activity_type: fishing | hunting | scuba | snow | water | adventure | tours

result_count: Zero-result identification — weekly report feeds directly to supply team for guide recruitment.

outcome: guide_card_clicked | booking_initiated | bounced | refined_search

privacy: No PII. Sanitisation strips email, phone, and name+location patterns from raw_query before storage.

Rollout timeline

Week 1
Alignment & engineering briefing. Mapping table drafted across 6 activity categories.
Week 2
Intent layer + browse state built. Query logging live from day one.
Week 3
Edge cases QA'd. Geo-intent validated across all 3 states (granted / denied / failed).
Week 4
First zero-result report to supply team. Unrecognised high-frequency terms flagged.

The Problem

A Semrush audit revealed 8 of our top 10 pages by traffic were species and blog pages — with zero booking CTAs. The top blog post (trout fishing guide) pulled 3.21% of all site traffic across 172 keywords. The page ended. Users hit back. They booked elsewhere.

Not a traffic problem. We had 16,900 monthly visitors with explicit transactional intent and no conversion path to meet them. A conversion architecture problem hiding as a growth problem.

SEO Audit Finding

61.8% of keywords are informational. 10% transactional — but those 10% drive 16,900 monthly visits. None had a booking path.

Root Cause

Template-level gap — blog and species page templates had no CTA wiring. One template change touches every page on the site.

Key Design Decision

Pre-filtered CTAs, not generic /search links. CTA links to /search?species=trout — matching exactly what the user was reading. Generic links don't convert.

High-Intent Moment

The habitat map on species pages = "where can I fish for this?" moment. Destination chips below the map, pulled from live guide inventory for that species.

Component Spec — Dynamic Guide Card Strip

placement: After 3rd content section — peak engagement point in reading session.

accepts params: species=trout | location=montana | activity_type=fly_fishing

renders: 3 guide cards — name, location, price, next available date, Check Availability CTA.

fallback: If <3 guides for species in region, broaden to state-level. Never render empty strip.

reused on: Blog template + Species template. One component, two page types. Zero duplication.

Case Study 03

WildCast: India's Outdoor Experience Marketplace

Independent Research Project · 2025–2026

Research project · Prototypes built
WILDCAST
"Book the Wild" — wildcast.in
MarketIndia outdoor experiences
ModelTwo-sided marketplace
VerticalsFishing · Trekking · Camping · Wildlife · Watersports · Snow
Target GMV Y3₹75 Crore

The opportunity

India has 1.4 billion people, extraordinary ecological diversity, and zero dominant platform for booking outdoor recreation experiences. Online penetration of activity booking is below 3% — 97% of bookings still happen over phone calls and WhatsApp forwards.

WildCast is an independent research and product exploration — market sizing, competitive analysis, India-specific product strategy, and two fully interactive prototypes, developed to validate whether a multi-vertical outdoor marketplace could work in the Indian context.

India-specific unlock

WhatsApp-native booking. Full booking flow inside WhatsApp — no app download required. No global outdoor platform has done this.

India-specific unlock

Informal guide formalisation. 500,000+ informal guides — mahouts, river guides, jungle trackers — who have never appeared on any platform.

India-specific unlock

Hybrid payment model. 20–30% online to confirm, balance in cash on arrival. Solves operator GST anxiety and consumer trust in one move.

Strategic differentiation

Multi-vertical from day one — fishing, trekking, camping, wildlife, watersports, and snow sports on a shared infrastructure. Corporate offsites as a dedicated high-ticket vertical. A conservation data layer (catch logs, sighting logs, footfall data) that compounds with every booking — creating a moat no pure-play marketplace can replicate.

Interactive prototypes — built without a designer

Consumer App

Full mobile experience: discovery, search by species/activity, listing pages, booking flow, and post-trip community features.

Built in Figma → HTML. Interactive — tap through the screens.

Operator Dashboard

Guide-facing web app: booking management, calendar, payout tracking, analytics, WhatsApp broadcast to past guests.

Fully interactive prototype — click through the tabs.

Both prototypes built independently — no designer, no agency. HTML + CSS + JS from scratch.
